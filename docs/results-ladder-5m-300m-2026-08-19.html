What tripling capacity actually bought

Six models from 4.75M to 296M body parameters, each instruction-tuned on the same corpus and scored on the same 5,024-question benchmark at a 32-token greedy budget. Overall accuracy rose 2.29% → 16.94%. Almost none of that came from the word-problem sets.

The headline curve hides the result

Plotted alone, overall accuracy looks like a clean power law: accuracy ∝ params0.445, rising steadily across the ladder. That curve is real but it is an average over five very different benchmarks, and the average conceals the thing worth knowing.

Three things the data says

1 · The ladder is not a clean experiment

The first three rungs trained at 100 tokens per parameter; the last three at 20. So 20M→50M is not a pure capacity step — it is 2.6× the parameters on half the tokens. That is very likely why 50M lands on top of 20M (9.16% → 9.43%, well inside noise) rather than above it. Read the plateau as a token-budget artifact, not evidence that capacity stopped paying.

2 · Capacity buys symbol manipulation, not comprehension

Algebra went 3 → 78 of 100 and arithmetic 1.33% → 44.33%, both scaling near params0.77. Over the same span ASDiv managed 2.73% → 16.96% and GSM8K 1.97% → 5.91%. The models are learning to execute procedures — carry a digit, isolate a variable — far faster than they learn to parse what a word problem is asking.

3 · GSM8K is a wall, and partly a measurement artifact

At ^0.278, GSM8K is nearly flat across a 62× capacity increase. Some of that is genuine multi-step reasoning difficulty. But some is the 32-token budget: GSM8K solutions routinely need more room than that, so the harness truncates answers the model might otherwise finish. The number is a floor, not a ceiling.

Confound worth naming

The 5M–100M rungs are single evaluations. The 300M figure is the best of a 3×3 seed/checkpoint grid whose readings spanned 601–851 correct (sd 79). Applying that variance to the lower rungs would likely blur the 20M-vs-50M ordering entirely. Treat mid-ladder rankings as provisional.

What it cost

Compute is where the ladder turns unfriendly. Pretraining time grew far faster than accuracy: the 300M rung consumed 92.8 GPU-hours — 78% of the ladder's total 118.6 — to move overall accuracy 4.36 points past 100M.

GPU-hours per benchmark question gained

Milli-GPU-hours per correct answer, i.e. total pretraining time divided by questions answered correctly. Lower is more efficient. The cost of a point rises 18× from 5M to 300M.

Where this leaves the next run

Held-out loss improved monotonically at every rung, 2.810 → 1.908, and never showed the plateau the benchmark did. Loss is still tracking capacity cleanly — it is the translation from loss to measurable capability that is uneven, and it is uneven in a specific direction.

  • Capacity is still the live axis for algebra and arithmetic, with no saturation visible at 300M. Both were still climbing steeply at the last rung.
  • Word problems need something other than parameters. Three rungs of capacity moved ASDiv barely 3 points. More scale is unlikely to be the cheapest fix.
  • Re-run GSM8K at 96 tokens before concluding anything about multi-step reasoning. The current number is confounded with truncation.
  • Fix the ladder's token budget before extending it. A 200M rung at 20 TPP would make 100M→200M→300M the first clean three-point capacity series in the set.
