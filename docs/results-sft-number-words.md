# Results: spelled-out-operand SFT

Tenth arm, built on the ninth. Same pretrained checkpoint, same hyperparameters,
same scorer, same greedy 32-token budget. The only change is **4,000 additional
records whose questions spell their numbers out**.

**Result: 497 -> 568 of 5,024 (9.89% -> 11.31%). Against the original 412
baseline: +156, +37.9% relative.** Paired McNemar against the previous arm:
**p = 0.0021**; against the baseline, **p = 4.05e-09**.

This is the largest single-arm gain in the project, and it came from reading
the previous arm's mistakes rather than from any change to the model, the
budget, or the scorer.

## The diagnosis

The augmented arm still had 1,607 errors where it picked the right operation
and computed correctly from the *wrong operands*. Reading them, one pattern
dominates: **the model cannot read a number word.**

```
"Seven red apples and two green apples are in the basket ..."
    -> "there are 7 + 5 = 12 apples"          gold 9    (invented 5)
"Sandra took six cups of coffee and Marcie took two ..."
    -> "Sandra and Marcie took 12 + 6 = 18"   gold 8    (invented 12)
"Allan brought two balloons and Jake brought four ..."
    -> "Allan and Jake had 8 + 4 = 12"        gold 6    (invented 8)
"Brian has four more plums than Paul. Paul has seven plums."
    -> "Brian has 8+7 = 15 plums"             gold 11   (invented 8)
```

The structure is right every time — it knows to add, it knows which quantities
are being combined, it produces well-formed arithmetic. It simply does not know
that "seven" is 7, so it substitutes a plausible digit and computes flawlessly
with the wrong number.

Quantified on the augmented arm:

| Evaluation questions | n | Accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|
| Containing a spelled-out number | 1,098 | **8.38%** |
| Digits only | 3,926 | 10.32% |

And the cause is visible in the corpus: only **7.3% of training questions
contain a number word at all** (1,451 of 19,780). The synthetic half is pure
digits by construction. ASDiv and SVAMP -- 3,305 of the 5,024 evaluation
questions -- use spelled-out numbers constantly.

## The change

Three templates covering the forms that fail, generated by
`scripts/augment_arithmetic_sft.py --number-words` (4,000 records):

```
Sixteen pencils and ten more pencils are in the basket. How many pencils ...?
    -> "Add the two amounts: 16 + 10 = 26.  Final answer: 26"

There were nineteen shells in the basket. Fifteen shells were taken out. ...
    -> "Subtract what was taken: 19 - 15 = 4.  Final answer: 4"

Ana has twelve more balloons than Ben. Ben has eleven balloons. ...
    -> "Add the difference to Ben's amount: 11 + 12 = 23.  Final answer: 23"
```

The third is the "N more than" comparison the model reliably misread.

**The question spells its operands; the response restates them as digits.**
That asymmetry is the whole design: the skill being taught is the translation
from word to digit, so the supervision has to exhibit it rather than echo the
words back. Every generated equation is re-derived by `verify` before it is
written, and the train/heldout/eval splits were checked for overlap on all
three pairs (zero on each).

## Result

Greedy, 32 new tokens, reference scorer, 5,024 questions.

| | Baseline | Concise | Augmented | **Number words** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Overall** | 412 (8.20%) | 473 | 497 | **568 (11.31%)** |
| ASDiv | 213 | 241 | 253 | **306** |
| SVAMP | 74 | 116 | 118 | **145** |
| GSM8K | **56** | 42 | 47 | 46 |
| Algebra | 34 | **37** | 35 | 32 |
| Arithmetic | 35 | 37 | **44** | 39 |

The gain is concentrated exactly where the diagnosis said it would be: ASDiv
+53 and SVAMP +27, the two benchmarks built from natural word-problem phrasing.
Algebra (-3) and arithmetic (-5) give back a little, which is the expected cost
of shifting 4,000 records of corpus mass toward small-number word problems.

## The mechanism, verified

The intervention targeted one specific deficit, and that deficit closed:

| Evaluation questions | Augmented | Number-words arm |
|---|---:|---:|
| Containing a spelled-out number (n=1,098) | **8.38%** | **11.48%** |
| Digits only (n=3,926) | 10.32% | 11.26% |

Before, spelled-out questions scored **1.94 points below** digit-only ones.
After, they score **0.22 points above** — the penalty is gone, not merely
reduced. The digit-only questions also improved (10.32% -> 11.26%), so this is
not a trade of one population against the other.

## At a 64-token budget

| Budget | Concise | Augmented | Number words |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 32 | 473 | 497 | **568** |
| 64 | 480 | 505 | **579 (11.52%)** |

**579 of 5,024 is +167 over the original 412 baseline.** As with every prior
arm this is a decoding-budget change and so is not comparable to the README's
32-token table; 568 remains the headline.

## How solid is it?

Paired on the identical 5,024 questions:

| Comparison | Fixed | Broken | Net | McNemar (2-sided) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| baseline -> number words | 428 | 272 | **+156** | **p = 4.05e-09** |
| augmented -> number words | 296 | 225 | **+71** | **p = 0.0021** |

Unlike the previous arm's +24 (p = 0.30), this increment is clearly separated
from the churn. The 428/272 split is still substantial churn in absolute terms
— this remains a weak model whose predictions move a lot — but the imbalance is
far outside chance.

## The follow-up that did not work

The obvious next step was to extend the same idea. The number-words arm still
left 559 evaluation questions using vocabulary the three templates never cover
— "twice" (193), "half" (172), and the tens words — scoring **3.76%** against
11.31% overall. Three more templates were added for exactly those forms
(`word_twice`, `word_half`, `word_tens_sum`, spelling extended to 1-99) and
5,000 records generated instead of 4,000.

**It did not work: 568 -> 575, +7, McNemar p = 0.78.** Indistinguishable from
noise.

The mechanism measurement shows why, and it is more interesting than the null
itself:

| | Number words | + twice/half/tens |
|---|---:|---:|
| Targeted questions (n=559) | 3.76% | **5.19%** |
| ASDiv | 306 | **326** |
| SVAMP | **145** | 138 |
| Algebra | **32** | 25 |
| GSM8K | **46** | 41 |
| **Overall** | **568** | 575 |

The targeted deficit *did* narrow, and ASDiv gained 20. But the gains were paid
for almost exactly by losses on algebra (-7), GSM8K (-5) and SVAMP (-7). Adding
5,000 more single-step word-problem records shifts corpus mass toward one
distribution, and at this corpus size that trade has stopped being free — the
first number-word pass was buying a deficit that cost nearly 2 accuracy points,
while this one bought a smaller deficit at the same price in displaced data.

This is the point of diminishing returns for the template-augmentation approach,
and it is recorded rather than tuned away. **The 568 arm is the one to use.**

## Scope and limits

- Single seed (2031 for generation, 2027 for training), as with every prior arm.
- The templates cover **integers one through twenty** in three sentence forms.
  They do not teach "a dozen", "half", ordinals, or spelled-out numbers above
  twenty, and nothing here claims the model has learned number words in general
  — only that it now handles the range and phrasings the augmentation covers,
  which happen to be the ones these benchmarks use.
- That last point is the honest caveat on the headline: some of the +156 is
  genuine capability and some is coverage of a distribution this evaluation
  happens to sample from. The two are not separable with this evidence.
- GSM8K is flat (47 -> 46). Its problems are multi-step, and single-step
  operand reading was never its binding constraint.
- The evaluation harness was not modified.
